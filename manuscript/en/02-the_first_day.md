The world was dying-- burning. So we built an ark to carry us into the future. We would sleep for centuries while the world healed, and dream of Utopia.
But our vessel failed one day, just for half of us, and we woke up to a world that no longer wanted us nor could hold us.
For the few that refused, this was like the dead rising up from their graves.
For the rest of us, the Yosemite-C was the dream and real world was Purgatory.
Where we belonged.

Leon Diaz-Abbatini

## The Fugue

[Montante](guide/people/monty.md) found her aboard the train after she was coming back on the second round trip. People around her said she went into a fugue state, that she seemed catatonic, but she knew it was just her [TDS](guide/glossary.md#tds) acting up. It was common for people to experience an episode after distressing news or moments of stress.  
Since then she had holed up in her new room, with [Miss Lina](guide/people/lina.md) leaving meals outside her door and then collecting them a couple hours later.  
Her throat was sore, her eyes burned and the skin of her face was painfully dry. She would just look at the ceiling fan going round and round, thinking of this and that, asking herself why she cared so much.
  
Montante had tried to see her a number of times, but relented when he got no response. No, not true, he would stay outside of her door until he made sure he could hear her slow and labored breathing, then he would leave. "He doesn't want to deal with another suicide".  
She struggled to make sense of why she cared so much. It wasn't the shock of the news, or whatever reasoning he might've had for doing it. It was the ridiculousness of it all and the theatrics. Monty was right, it was just like him to do this.  
So why did she care? And why did she care so much?

This past "mentor" of hers had barely mattered. She barely knew him for 2 years, and he mostly taught her how to conduct boring surveys of people going into the [Yosemite-C](guide/glossary.md#yosemite-continuum).  
So why did she care? Why care for this old, grumpy man who was drunk half the time she saw him and who had the nerve to call her to another country, away from her work and her life, only to commit suicide as theater. "He probably only did it because he knew I was coming".

Determined to move on from this chapter, and to not give him the sick satisfaction he so desired, she decided to use the tools from the [somatic override](guide/glossary.md#somatic-override) practice to leave the mess behind. So she closed her eyes and…tap, tap, tap…it was gone. It was a new day and she didn't have time to sit around and mope.

## Back in Action

She walked down to the first floor and found [Miss Lina](guide/people/lina.md), the elderly woman who owned the house picking up her plate from the table as Monty continued eating breakfast.  
She greeted Natalia and carried on with her day, Monty was dumbstruck.  
"I hope you had a good nap", said Miss Lina.

— I…did?

— You seem well.

Monty had a silly smile on his face. His mountainous figure took up most of the tiny room in between the kitchen and the living room. The house was quaint and she wasn't sure about the details of the arrangement. They arrived in the middle of the night and she hadn't had much interaction with Miss Lina, other than coldly refusing the meals she made for her.

— Sorry, let me help you with this\!

— No need, girl. You should get ready instead. It's your first day of school, remember?

— Ah, no, that's on Monday…

— What day do you think it is? All that spacing out didn't do you any favors. You won't be getting that time back.

— That's a delicate subject, Nana…

Miss Lina was clearly bothered, and Natalia wasn't surprised. She knew that out here, [TDS](guide/glossary.md#tds) wasn't taken very seriously and someone older like Miss Lina probably viewed it with skepticism. Or so she thought. She then realized it was said as sorrowful advice. "She must be a [Returner](guide/glossary.md#returners). 4, 6…8 years?", Natalia thought. She imagined what it must've been like for an older woman like her to wake up after 8 years, in an older, more fragile body.

She decided to focus on the task at hand. [Dr. Leon](guide/people/leon.md) left a final wish for both her and Monty, apart from the binder. Apparently he had been teaching at a local school and he wanted them to continue with his classes, until a replacement was found.

— You don't have to start today…

— No, I'm ready.

— You don't even know what subject you're supposed to be teaching.

— Is it not Psychology?

— Oh no…

It was History, her weakest subject. She started to feel flushed and nervous, but she knew she could get by for the first day if she just studied the material properly.

— Of course…just lend me the syllabus and his class notes. Leon may have been a mess in his personal life, but he was nothing if not organized.

— I don't know how helpful these will be…

He handed her a binder full of pages in disarray. She tried not to let the anxiety take over and instead laid them all out on the table. She took a step back and allowed her brain to take in the information, then closed her eyes and thought of a familiar place. Her Austin apartment. She didn't have time, so something familiar would do. She started arranging the concepts and a minute later, opened her eyes to find Monty looking at her sternly with his mouth open.  

— Monty: Is this a Continuum thing?

— Natalia: No, just a memory palace. It's a basic technique along with maximizing throughput for--

— Lina: You're teaching teenagers, how much talking do you really think you'll get to do?

She was right, but she didn't want to mess it up. It was her first day and she needed to set the expectations right, after all.

## Arriving at the school

She and Monty took the train together. He was also bound to the dying wish of a man neither one of them remembered fondly, but he fared better, he would be teaching botanics, a subject that was on the brink of being cut, apparently.

The school was much bigger than she had expected. An ex-convent turned into [Marymount](guide/timeline.md#marymount), a private school, it was surrounded by trees and full of colonial architecture with big wooden doors.
It had clearly come a long way, since there were people working on further restorations all around.

— This is it. The Marymount. I will give you a tour after classes are done. I think you'll enjoy the hidden passageways, even if we can't go in.

— Hidden passageways?

— They say they connect to the city center, more than a century old. But that's just what people like to say.

It was hard to find architecture this old in Austin. Most of the buildings were modernized, with a small collective working to protect the historical buildings. Her city didn't appreciate anything that wasn't from the 30s, it seemed. At least they still had Scholz.

Something else caught her attention.

— I…was not expecting this.

— Students?

— No…they all look the same age, roughly.

— Were you expecting them to be Remainers?

— No, well, maybe? I read the statistics. It's roughly 40/60 in [Cuernavaca](guide/people/groups/cuernavaca.md).

— Yes, well…most students here are Returners. I'd say it's around 20/80.

She could see the discomfort in his face. If the split was different with students than the general population…she wondered where the rest of the students this age were going to school.

— …I imagine you'll be more at ease here. They try to emulate your teaching style. That's probably why Leon was brought onboard in the first place. They will appreciate having you here.

She could see the logic. The [Coalition](guide/glossary.md#coalition) had made great strides in reintegrating both camps in Austin, primarily by using an advanced curriculum from the [2030s](guide/chronology.md#gilded-age) that got everyone up to speed.
She wished she had access to that curricula now.

She felt a great distance between them. He didn't have 10 years of his life missing. She felt a knot in her throat starting to form, so she decided to move on.

— N- So where do we register for our class?

— M- Here's your teaching plan.

Natalia felt embarrassed. She was always on top of everything, especially planning a class. Maybe coming here was a mistake.

— M- Are you okay?

— N- Of course.

— M- I don't believe you. Come on, talk to me. Aren't you the therapist?

— N- Solving your own issues isn't as easy…if only I could clone myself.

— M- I can't do a convincing impression of you, but…what would you do if I was struggling instead?

— N- I would say that you're starting a new job in another country after a tragic loss. That this is not the time to be hard on yourself, but rather to be open to new opportunities.

— M- That's a damn good impression of good therapy. For a moment I thought you'd tell me to just swallow some overpriced pills.

— N- Haha, no, but some of my colleagues would. I try to start with understanding the root of the problem before trying to solve the symptoms.

— M- Good luck here. You're going to have many symptoms to deal with.

They approached the Principal's office. It overlooked a large courtyard with a well in the middle and some palm trees on the side. She felt ready to join the school life.

## Meeting the Principal

The wait was starting to make her nervous. Her class was supposed to start in just a few minutes. She noticed the technology used by the school might be just as old as the buildings, they were still using technology from decades ago and were unable to move away from paper. She wondered if the paper supply issues were also present in Cuernavaca.

They moved on to the Principal's office after a long wait. Natalia's class was meant to start in 20 minutes but she was still waiting. She would probably arrive late to her first day after all. Monty stayed with her, even though his gardening class was not scheduled until the afternoon.  

— M- Strange, huh?

— N- That she said she'd be right with us and it's been 5 minutes?

— M- I'm not surprised. People don't see 5 minutes here like they do in Austin.

— N- Then what were you referring to?

— M- Being here again. I haven't seen you in 15 years…and we're back in high school. It's like when we met.

— N- …

— M- I'm sorry. I realize it doesn't feel like 15 years to you.

— N- It's okay...

She tried to stop thinking about her missing decade and started tapping lightly on her head.

— N- Yes, for me it would only be 5 years. I've found peace with my missing years.

— M- ...How is Austin after all these years?

— N- Very high-paced, is right. The first years were very messy, but we've been doing well since then. They finally restored the building in Lavaca.

— M- The chilli place?

— N- Now it's a salad and chilli place.

— M- Awful. …and how are you in Austin?

— N- Good. I really like my job. I work for Apex Galilei and I'm an L42, so I should be able to make director in 3 years if I get my publication approved. Being here should help with the wait.

— M- And that means?

— N- Oh, I'm a corporate therapist. I help employees reach their maximum potential. And I do a good job.

— M- You help them with their [TDS](guide/glossary.md#tds)?

— N- Yes, almost everyone in Austin struggles with it. I'm investigating some promising approaches.

— M- Can I ask...what it is like?

— N- Yes. It's a condition where a missing neurological path triggers--

— M- No, I meant...for you.

— N- Oh. It's much better now. I just avoid thinking about my time in the [Yosemite-C](guide/glossary.md#yosemite-continuum).

— M- I guess sometimes it's best to just bury those memories.

— N- Not quite, rather than burying it's just knowing those memories lead nowhere. It's like not pulling on a loose thread from your sweater, or else it will unravel.

— M- "It" being your mind?

— N- Haha, precisely.

— M- So it's just like one big dream you can't remember?

— N- Yes...a bittersweet dream.

The Principal comes out. She has big hair and is wearing a lot of jewelry.

— P- Hello, hello. I'm the Principal, [Lomeli Mendez](guide/people/mendez.md). I must say, what a pleasure to have you here! You must be Natalia Sunday! To have one of Dr. Leon's apprentices with us\!

— M- And I'm Baltazar Montante, I was also--

— P- Yes, yes. I believe you've taught here before. You must be familiar with the school already. But Miss Sunday here must be exhausted! Was it a long ride all the way from Austin?

— N- It was, but everyone has been so welcoming.

— P- Hopefully not more than us, haha. We here at Marymount pride ourselves on our forward-thinking and tech-savvy approach to teaching. Losing Leon was such a tragedy, but he was also set in his ways. Getting a bright young mind like yours will be such a joy for the students. They are all lovely, kind spirits.

— N- Thank you for such a warm welcome. I will do my best, my background is in Psychology but I've studied the syllabus for the History class and I can assure you, I will only get better from here on out.

— P- Of course you will, you're from the Republic of Texas! Tell me, did you bring any of those...gadgets you put on to study while you sleep?

— N- Oh no, sadly I didn't... I wasn't prepared. But I'll make sure to bring some from Austin soon.

— P- You are such a doll! I'll tell you, if you do, I'll see what I can do to change your subject to college-level Psychology once you've gotten the children to master the basics. If they learn while they sleep, they should finish high school in, what, 4-6 months?

— N- It's not quite--

— P- Of course, of course, take your time...but don't waste any \! Haha, you know what they say, haste makes waste, but there is no waste like the time spent in waiting \! Now, off you go and best of luck on your first day, dear\! Call for me at any sign of distress\!

— N and M- ...

She rushes them out of her office, the two stare at each other, dumbfounded.

— M- Do you think she's on something? She's like a puppy that got into the wrong bag of powder.

— N- She made me feel back in Austin, actually.

She ran and made it 2 minutes late to the class. It really was much faster to just turn right.

## The first class

— N- Hello students. I am Natalia Sunday. I will be your substitute teacher for today and for the rest of the semester.

Everyone stood up and started chanting in unison, "Hello, miss Natalia." Then they sat down.

— N- As you have likely heard by now, your former professor passed away tragically…

Nobody moved, they didn't even react. Did they know? This might be easier than she expected.

— N- Your syllabus says you were learning about the Gilded Age.

Not a single voice was heard. She was unsure if her translation box was working properly.

— N- You all can hear me, right? I was told you all have [t-boxes](guide/glossary.md#t-box), or that you all speak English if not…

Some students nodded here and there, so she took it to mean she was good to go. But where should she start? She recalled the images and concepts of the lecture after closing her eyes for a second, and then prepared to start…but there it was. Monty was telling her what had happened to Leon and she started to feel a wave building up inside her.

She realized she was being overcome by the mess again, so she closed her eyes for another moment and…tap, tap, tap. The classroom was merely an assignment now. The students were looking at her in confusion, but she didn't care. She carried on with the lecture.

Natalia didn’t look at the students. She looked at the dust motes dancing in the shafts of light from the high colonial windows.

— N-"The [2030s](guide/chronology.md#gilded-age) were a period of great economic development and social unrest that was just as big" she said, her voice sounding like a recording even to herself. "Some refer to this as [The Gilded Age](guide/chronology.md#gilded-age), some others as [The Great Decay](guide/chronology.md#gilded-age). We all know what it led to in 2039, but let's first examine the conditions and circumstances that lead some to believe this was the greatest decade of the world."

The lecture continued without a hitch, she was able to deliver the whole plan, remembering each note in the notebook with pinpoint accuracy. Her training had prepared her well. With a confident face, she said

— N- That is all. You may leave now.

Just as the analog bell rang.

## After the first day

She reconvenes with Monty. They ride the train back.  

— M- How did the first day go?

— N- I think it was perfect? I might have a calling for teaching.

— M- I am amazed. I was worried you were going to get a rowdy class.

— N- No, they were so well-behaved. Not a single peep. How about yours?

— M- Terrible, as expected. But I managed. They're good kids. At least Leon's tasks weren't worse. Actually, I need to go to the Hacienda tomorrow to organize his things. Would you like to come? Hopefully there's no secret letter with the real, awful tasks.

— N- Sure--

Faint memories of Leon started to well up. Thinking of going through his things made her incredibly sad, then a rage started building. She quickly tuned it out.

— M- Natalia? Are you okay?

— N- Yes, yes. It's just some TDS.

— M- It's okay to be upset, you know? I might not like the bastard, but I still mourn him. He wasn't always like this...

— N- I'm doing so well as a teacher. I don't need to deal with this right now.

— M- No, I'm not saying you have to deal with it right now, but don't bottle up those emotions. You must know better than anyone how unhealthy it can be.

— N- Yes...I would just like to leave it for later.

— M- I understand that. Just don't use your therapy hacks to push it down. You're off the clock now, if you just want to stay home and blob, I can cook dinner for you.

— N- No, you're right. I'll rest tonight and recoup my energy for tomorrow.

— M- You do that. Rely on your hacks if need be, but don't do it too much. These are good kids, it might be worthwhile to connect with them, rather than just lecture them.

## The second class

The next day, she started the lecture like she did the first time. She didn't want to let the intrusive thoughts pop in, but perhaps she could be more present to learn about her students. She was going to be doing this for the upcoming months, so perhaps collecting some more data and understanding her students could be beneficial.
She didn't rely on the override this time. She trusted herself to focus on the concepts and the notes she has studied.  
She started noticing something odd…the students were constantly tapping at their desk with their fingers. She carried on.  
In the middle of a sentence, 3 students started laughing out of nowhere. She stopped and they shut up. The tapping intensified. Perhaps this was a way in which the younger generation was dealing with stress. After all, they were teenagers undergoing puberty and new to the 10th grade. She was about to remember what it must've been like for her, but her training stopped her on the spot. "This could trigger an episode". She knew by now that reflecting on experiences as old as this, could have some very bad side effects. And she wasn't going to let her perfect streak end. So she composed herself and carried on.

In the middle of the lecture, she noticed a girl in the corner looking irritated.

N- Excuse me, miss. Is there something that didn't land well?

The girl looked around to make sure she was being addressed. "No, no. 2030s. Chaos and corporations booming. I got it all. No notes." She said, withdrawing back into her hoodie after she was done. There was more tapping on the desks and snickering. Natalia found this odd.

N- Ok, let me know if you have any questions.

While everyone was leaving, she approached the girl and asked.

N- Is everything ok? I noticed you looked a little…off.

Girl- No, you can continue.

All of a sudden, the other students burst out in laughter. The girl looked upset.

N- Did I miss something?

The girl rolled her eyes and the tapping continued. It was just registering for her, but she realized it had been going on for the whole class.
She decided to keep going with her lecture but now the tapping became ubiquitous. She could not ignore it. And she started noticing the glancing, the repressed laughter, the smirks.
They were not listening to her lecture, they were making fun of her.

Memories of her teenage years started coming up, feelings of embarrassment of being just a girl. The emotions overwhelmed her and she tried to connect with her training. She remembered Leon and her years learning under him, the years--the months... When was this? Did she really study under Leon? Where else did she feel like these?
She felt like a thread of memories had gotten stuck on a ring and her emotions were pulling faster and faster on it. She tried to remember her younger years, she tried to remember when she finally broke through that early insecurity and found...nothing. There was a vast chasm in her mind, between who she used to be and who she was now. She tried anchoring herself with her training, but what training? She was just a girl. She was 21--no, 26, no, 31, no, 37. She was 37-years-old\!
She was not much older than these kids just a few years ago and now she was a grown woman\! With no memories, with no growth, with nothing but decline ahead of her\!
She felt her stomach drop, like she had just opened her eyes on a rollercoaster, just before the scariest and highest of falls.
There was none of her training left and she felt a familiar wind pick up.

Girl- Miss...are you okay?

She opened her eyes just as she started to feel her eyes lift off. She knew she wouldn't be able to stop it now, so she took the time still remaining to her and she sprinted off to the door, down the hall and as far as she could go. She couldn't know where she stopped, but she was sure that she had. She was probably just a mindless husk in the middle of the hallway by now. She knew that panicking and trying to come back would make it worse, so she allowed herself to unravel first.
Like a wave taking over her, she had to wait for the tumbling to stop before she could find the way out.
She was ready for the whirlwind to make way to a vastness of red, when she felt something on her shoulder.
Her shoulder? She was able to connect with the rest of her body just by finding her one shoulder, until she eventually found her own gaze.

She was sitting on some stairs, face cradled by her arms and knees, with tears streaming down her face. She had never had an episode like this. She lifted her face and turned to her right.

Girl- There, there.

The girl from before was caressing her shoulder, like someone who was trying to pet a stinky cat.
She cleaned off her tears and looked at her, but no words came out.

Girl- They're so lame. You shouldn't waste your tears on them.

The girl had curly black hair, medium length, and she was wearing a bandana. She sat down next to Natalia, knees up, just like her.

Girl- They're always like this. I'm surprised you noticed so quickly. They were just starting to ramp up. 

N- I can't even explain what they were doing...but they were...

Girl- Making fun of you? Yes, obviously. They're using my haptic glove.

N- Your...?

Girl- It's this stupid thing I made. It's paired gloves communicating over short-range bluetooth, kinetic signals and micromovements convert to stored representations in the t-box which is then broadcasted to the surroundings. You don't *have* to pair your gloves, but it helps with latency. The important thing is the embeddings representation in their t-box that allows them to read it as a group chat.

N- I don't follow. They have a group chat?

Girl- Yes, basically they were making fun of you in the class group chat.

N- You too?

Girl- No, I'm...not officially part of the group chat. I can still read their stupid frequency, though. They're so stupid, they think I don't understand any of it.

She put her head on her knees, with a sad gaze in her eyes.

N- Do they also make fun of you in their group chat?

Girl- ...

Her eyes seemed to be welling up.

N- I'm sorry...

Girl- Ugh, like I said. They're stupid. I hope you don't quit just because of this, or die. Please don't die. It would be so lame if you died because of this.

N- Do you think Leon died because of this?

Girl- I don't know! They didn't tell us anything! All I know, is he was my favorite teacher and he's dead now, and you're not a half-bad teacher and it would suck if you died too, okay? I actually like your robot-mode lectures...

N- Haha...I won't. I'll be there tomorrow. Thank you...

Girl- It's whatever.

N- I'm Natalia.

Girl- I know, you're our teacher. But yeah, I'm Paloma. Anyways, I need to get back to your class, which is almost over. Do you even remember what class you were in?

N- Haha, yes. I'll see you tomorrow, Paloma.

Paloma- Later, *Tich*\!

She went back to pick her things and walked in, back straight, into the classroom. She faced the classroom and took the time to look into each of their faces. It was the first time she was really looking into them. She saw past the smirks, the mocking looks and the general expectation to see if she would break again.
These were just kids. There were 8 of them. To the left, a girl with a shy smile, a dress and elaborate braids, then a serious girl wearing a track suit and long, straight hair, then a very tall boy on the first row. In the second row, an empty desk, then a girl with short hair and very detailed makeup, next to a boy with short hair and a prominent nose with an impish smile.
In the back row, there was a boy in a hoodie staring out the window with sleepy eyes, a bored boy with long hair, and finally Paloma in a corner.

She stared for a moment longer and then said.
N- You're dismissed, see you tomorrow.

They all waited for a moment, until one of them got up and the rest did. She focused on who left with whom and who stayed behind, Paloma was the first to exit the classroom, with the girl in makeup and the one with the tracksuit last, not without first giving her a glance.

It wasn't much, but it was everything she needed to understand. Paloma was being pushed out, and there was someone holding complete control of the group.
She felt a ping of excitement in her chest. She wasn't very popular in her high school days, but she had become very observant. She knew now who was driving the bullying and how to break it apart. She just needed to dig into one of her favorite subjects.
She couldn't wait to get home and review her notes.
It was time to teach Group Theory.

## At the Hacienda 

It's an old building. With plants coming in from the outside. A mix of technology and old architecture.  

Monty and Natalia had been going through the different things scattered around, putting them in bags to sort out later. For now, they just needed the place to not reek of Leon.

M- We should be about done now. I just need to move this chest later.

N- Really? You made it seem like there was much more.

M- Well...I didn't want you to deal with it, you've already helped out a lot. It's just the lab that's left?

N- There's a lab?

M- Yes, it's underground, hidden--

N- Of course.

M- Right. That's actually where I found him. His remains.

N- Then yes, I want to help. You shouldn't be the only one having to deal with this.

M- Are you sure? You're already having to adapt to a new country, a new school, the news--

N- Thanks for worrying, but let's just get it over with.

They go to the basement, it looks like a typical, cramped cellar. Monty puts his hand on the wall, takes a deep breath and breathes out on the wall surface. It slides around to reveal small vines that were forming that section of the wall. They revealed a much bigger chamber, where the lab is. It's full of vials, strange contraptions, plants and machinery.


N- What was that?

M- [Haliatus mycelium](guide/glossary.md#haliatus). We were studying the technology together. It's a type of fungus that reacts to the bio-signature of the breath. It's not very secure, but he always liked his games.

N- A fake wall and a hidden lab sounds right up his alley.

M- Exactly. I've been to this lab before, but I didn't think much of the wall at first, I figured he had just blocked off the bad memories.

N- He didn't have a great time here, huh?

M- Just look around. The place is covered in notes and misery. I stopped working with him a few years ago. He spent most of his time cooped up after that.

N- I wonder if his message to me was a cry for help...

M- I'd rather we don't keep discussing the subject. I tried to help him, I really did.

N- I know... He did leave something for me that relies on that technology. The...fungus thing. It's a conch that turns into a book of sorts. It looked like straight out of a fantasy story!

M- Like so many things with Leon did. He was the master of smoke and mirrors. He really paved the road to hell...

Natalia decided to stop prying. She realized Monty was trying to open up for her sake, but there were many memories he'd rather not touch. Just being here should stir up a lot of pain for him.

N- Actually, why don't you help me out with something? We can sort out the lab later.

M- What is it?

N- My students. I had a slip up today but it led to me connecting with one of them, a girl, Paloma.

M- Paloma Soliz? I know her. Very smart.

N- *Very*. But she's being bullied, and there's a strange dynamic in the classroom.

M- It's likely that she's a Remainer and the other kids are Returners.

N- Maybe, I need to test out some hypotheses first.

Natalia got out her notebook and started drawing the seating arrangement.

She had 8 students and 9 desks. Paloma was in the corner and Daniela, who was always dressed up and Valeria's best friend in the middle. Clearly the queen bee, she thought.

She is the queen bee, but even at a distance, she kept her grip on Paloma, on the whole class, really. Halfway through the class, she told Santiago to go "fetch her a book".
