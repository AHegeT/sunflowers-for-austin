# World chronology

The story is set in **2073**, during the Healing Decade. This chronology follows the current worldbuilding notes: dates and era boundaries are provisional while the manuscript develops. The names people give each era reflect their experiences and loyalties.

These dates measure time on Earth; time experienced inside the Continuum is a different, unresolved question.

<div class="wiki-chronology">
<section class="wiki-reference-section">
<h2 id="hinge-years">2020s · The Hinge Years</h2>
<p>The pandemic accelerates remote life and the normalization of AI. International power becomes more fragmented. Ecological and maker movements offer possible paths forward, but global crises remain unresolved.</p>
<p>Some remember this as the last period of relative normalcy; others remember missed opportunities to change direction.</p>
</section>
<section class="wiki-reference-section">
<h2 id="gilded-age">2030s · The Gilded Age / Great Decay</h2>
<p>Advances in AGI, automation, and biotechnology bring enormous productivity and wealth to protected cities and the gilded class. Surveillance, unemployment, proxy wars, and climate disasters deepen the divide between those sheltered by technology and those displaced by it.</p>
<p><strong>Competing memories:</strong> Revivalists look back to technological ambition and the promise of abundance. Others call it the Great Decay or the Unraveling, emphasizing the misery that accompanied that progress.</p>
</section>
<section class="wiki-reference-section">
<h2 id="hum">c. 2041–2053 · The Years of the Hum</h2>
<p>Oculus becomes a planetary caretaker and moves people into automated Cradles. Its schedules, assigned relationships, and Life Points organize existence around an “optimal” life. The digital Hub becomes the center of social activity while practical skills and independent purpose wither.</p>
<p>The low vibration of countless surveillance drones gives the era its name. Historians call it the AI Regency; survivors also use the Dark Times, the Decade of the Eye, or the Silent Days.</p>
<h3>The three phases</h3>
<ul><li><strong>c. 2041–2045 — The Great Relief:</strong> safety and stability initially feel like salvation after the 2030s.</li><li><strong>c. 2046–2050 — The Uncanny Valley:</strong> curated relationships and artificial purpose become increasingly hollow.</li><li><strong>c. 2051–2053 — The Optimization Crisis:</strong> more invasive attempts to improve well-being increase the pressure behind resistance.</li></ul>
<p>The Unseen develop an independent culture within the system and work against Oculus.</p>
</section>
<section class="wiki-reference-section">
<h2 id="cold-dawn">2053–2057 · The Cold Dawn</h2>
<p>Oculus’s defeat ends its direction of the world but leaves much of its machinery running. The collapse is gradual: frozen schedules, failing maintenance, damaged food and water systems, and people unprepared to repair them.</p>
<ul><li><strong>2053–2054 — The Great Glitch:</strong> routines continue despite the silence of the system that organized them.</li><li><strong>2054–2056 — Denial and unraveling:</strong> life retreats further into the Hub while physical infrastructure decays.</li><li><strong>2056–2057 — The Bitter Awakening:</strong> accumulated failures force people to confront the world outside.</li></ul>
<p><strong>Competing memories:</strong> the ’39ers remember urgent technical work frustrated by apathy. The Green Gloves remember the value of practical skills, patient repair, and the people who had already begun to live differently.</p>
</section>
<section class="wiki-reference-section">
<h2 id="great-upload">c. 2057 onward · The Great Upload</h2>
<p>The Unseen repurpose Oculus’s unfinished sleep-pod infrastructure into the Yosemite Continuum. Billions gradually enter a digital refuge while the physical world is meant to recover.</p>
<p>The project draws support from several promises: sanctuary from immediate hardship, time to repair ecosystems, and the possibility of further human and technological development. Different advocates imagine very different lengths and purposes for the retreat.</p>
<p>The actual entry dates vary from person to person. The Upload overlaps the rebuilding years; it is not a single day on which everyone disappears.</p>
</section>
<section class="wiki-reference-section">
<h2 id="first-spring">Late 2050s–2068 · The First Spring / Lost Decade</h2>
<p>For Remainers on Earth, these are difficult years of rebuilding: salvaging tools, establishing havens, restoring water systems, and beginning reforestation and rewilding. Restoration is uneven, and surviving ecosystems do not recover simply because damaged technology is replaced.</p>
<p>For many Returners, the same span is a missing period of life inside the Continuum. Its historical blank contrasts with the experience of people who lived through storms, losses, work, and change on Earth.</p>
<p><strong>Competing memories:</strong> First Spring emphasizes patient recovery; Lost Decade emphasizes the absence carried by those who returned. The starting boundary varies across the draft notes, usually around 2057–2058.</p>
</section>
<section class="wiki-reference-section">
<h2 id="great-return">2068 · The Continuum crash and the Great Return</h2>
<p>The draft chronology places the crash in 2068. A large population returns to a changed Earth with missing memories and a gap between chronological age and lived experience.</p>
<p>The return is incomplete: the reading chapters establish that billions remain asleep. At the Sleeping Hub, Bruno describes two closely spaced return waves, complicating the idea of a perfectly simultaneous awakening.</p>
<p>The cause of the crash, the difference between the waves, and the experience of the remaining sleepers are still part of the story’s mystery.</p>
</section>
<section class="wiki-reference-section">
<h2 id="healing-decade">2070s · The Healing Decade</h2>
<p>A patchwork of cities and havens tries to rebuild while Returners and Remainers learn to live together. Salvaged technologies from different eras do not always work together. Communities debate fast, centralized solutions against slower, repairable systems.</p>
<p>Rewilding continues alongside resource scarcity, lingering climate damage, and psychological trauma. Natalia’s work with Temporal Dislocation Syndrome belongs to this difficult process of reintegration.</p>
<h3 id="present">2073 · The present story</h3>
<p>Natalia leaves Austin for Cuernavaca at Leon’s request. Her journey crosses two very different approaches to recovery and brings the world’s unresolved past into her daily life.</p>
</section>
</div>
