## T-Box
A "translation box", basically a smart phone with limited functionality. It serves as a personal knowledge repository and has local AI capabilities with a neural network that adjusts to the user. They are very valuable depending on the information it has. You need to tune into one and they are usually encrypted.
Very common to use one with one earbud to translate things in real time or recall important information.
Looks like a gemstone and projects a holographic UI.
They tend to not have broad internet functionality in Mexico for fear of being hacked or exposed. In Austin, they have intranet connection.


## Temporal Dislocation Syndrome
Like a violent deja vu.
Usually experienced as a "drop" like in a dream or a whirlwind effect when everything starts spinning around you. It feels like you have the emotional resonance of an event but none of the narrative or cohesive elements.
There is a tremendous emotional void that threatens to consume you if you stare at it too hard.
Some experience temporal "stutter" where they experience something in seconds but minutes happen to other observers. This can happen without warning and in the middle of actions.

Some cases experience an emotional flatlining, where they're unable to connect to emotions or loved ones. They are polite but slow and distant.
They experience proprioceptive dysphoria, where they feel disconnected from their bodies. Some forget to feed themselves or others.
Some symptoms are similar to dementia.

In worse cases, patients can be catatonic or have episodes in the middle of walking or driving. They can spend all day sitting on a bench, caught in an episode.

Studies have shown that grounding techniques like tapping on the vagus nerve, coarse sandpaper, extreme shifts in temperature, pungent smells or shocks seem to help.
The different degrees are called anchorage:
- Tier I: Residual
	- These people are the most common. They didn't stay in the YC long or had high mental resilience. They have "ghost limb"-like experiences, where they might walk into a room and feel a powerful urge to talk to someone who isn't there.
	- Like a constant feeling of having forgotten an appointment that never existed.
- Tier II: Dissociative
	- They have enough "hardware" left to cook, clean, and work, but the emotional emotional connection is lost. Because they lived subjective centuries in the YC, their current life feels like a "low-resolution" sequel to a masterpiece they can't remember.
	- They aren't catatonic, but they are emotionally anemic. They don't react to tragedy or joy because their internal scale for "importance" was calibrated by thousands of years of digital stimuli.
- Tier III: Fractal
	- This is a dangerous, unstable state. The brain is actively trying to "index" the missing 10 years and failing. This causes the "Whirlwind Effect" multiple times a day.
	- A person might be mid-sentence and then simply stop. Their pupils dilate, and their heart rate spikes to 150 BPM. To them, they are experiencing a century of compressed static. When they "land" back in reality, they are often physically exhausted and terrified.
- Tier IV: Terminal
	- The brain has given up. The "emotional void" has finally consumed the narrative self. These individuals are physically healthy but mentally "empty." They don't respond to pain, hunger, or their own names.
	- They aren't dreaming; they are suspended. Their consciousness is caught in a loop of trying to access a memory file that has been deleted, leaving them in a permanent state of "loading."
