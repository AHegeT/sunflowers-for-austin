# 2030s revivalists or '39ers
They believe that the 2030s or the Gilded Age was taking the right path until the AI God came along. They believe that corporations were right in creating safe havens for people and that technology had the answer. They are usually wealthy and kept safe by the corporations.
## **Architecture:**
Sleek, minimalist towers of smart-glass and steel, connected by sky-bridges. Parametric design, flowing lines, and integrated vertical farms climbing the sides of arcologies. It's a clean, efficient, and often sterile environment.
## **Technology:**
They embrace salvaged tech with gusto. Public spaces have augmented reality overlays displaying news and art. Transport is silent and electric. Their primary goal is to get the pre-Collapse energy grids and automated factories back online.
## **Lifestyle:**
The culture is fast-paced, goal-oriented, and hyper-connected through their local data networks. There's a deep-seated "cult of productivity," a collective anxiety about making up for the ten years they lost. They are builders, innovators, and workaholics.
## **The Psychology:**
To understand the '39ers is to understand the core trauma of a Returner. They feel like they were robbed of a decade of their prime. The '39er movement is a powerful coping mechanism, offering them a chance to not just rebuild the world, but to reclaim their own lost time by accelerating the future. They are deeply optimistic about what they can build but profoundly naive about the systemic risks that led to the first collapse.

# 2020s revivalists or Green Gloves
## Philosophy: "Healing is Work"
The core belief of the Green Gloves is that humanity cannot think or innovate its way out of the crisis it created. The only solution is through direct, patient, physical labor. Their central tenet is **"Healing is Work."** This applies to everything: healing the soil requires composting and planting (work), healing the community requires building and sharing (work), and healing the individual soul requires engaging in tangible, purposeful tasks (work). They believe the '39ers, with their focus on automation and grand designs, are trying to skip the most essential step: the slow, humbling process of physically repairing the damage.
## Architecture: "Adaptive Biology"
Green Gloves architecture is never built _on_ the land, but _with_ it. It is a direct rejection of the sterile, imposing towers of the Revivalist cities.
- **Adaptive Reuse:** Their first principle is to never build new if an old structure can be adapted. They hollow out the concrete husks of pre-Collapse buildings, turning them into vertical farms and residential blocks, weaving nature back into the ruins. A ruined skyscraper in their hands doesn't get torn down; it becomes a trellis for a vast network of gardens.
- **Biomaterials:** New structures are made from living or locally sourced materials. They use fast-growing bamboo, rammed earth, and salvaged materials. Their most advanced technique is the use of cultivated **mycelium bricks**, which are lightweight, incredibly strong, and biodegradable.
- **Integration with Nature:** Buildings are designed to work with the environment. They feature passive solar heating and cooling, rainwater harvesting systems built into the walls, and integrated greenhouses. A Green Gloves settlement should feel like a ruin that has been reclaimed by a vibrant, organized, and intentional forest.### Architecture: "Adaptive Biology"
## Technology: "Appropriate and Repairable"
The Green Gloves are not Luddites—they are brilliant engineers. But their definition of "good" technology is completely different from the '39ers.
- **The Toolbox:** Their technology is human-scale, durable, and, above all, **repairable**. They favor things like:
    - Robust, componetized mechanisms over delicate electronics (e.g., hand-cranked water pumps and mills).
    - Open-source, ruggedized 3D printers for making repair parts, not disposable goods.
    - Local, decentralized mesh networks for communication, not a single, vulnerable grid.
    - Solar-powered tools and small-scale hydroelectric generators.
- **The Maker Hub:**
	- Like the Makers, their social and technological center is the community "Maker Hub." This is where the revered "Master Makers" teach the young how to weld, code simple microcontrollers, repair salvaged tech, and cultivate bio-materials. Every piece of technology is designed to be understood and maintained by the community that uses it.
## Lifestyle: "Patient and Purposeful"
The daily life of someone in a Green Gloves haven is defined by purposeful work and deep community bonds.
- **The Rhythm of the Sun:** Life is organized around natural cycles rather than a 24/7 digital clock. The workday is often communal, focused on shared projects like tending the fields, maintaining the water purifiers, or expanding a settlement.
- **Skill over Status:** Social status is derived entirely from practical skills and a willingness to contribute. A master composter or a skilled water engineer is held in higher esteem than a charismatic speaker.
- **Celebration of Tangibility:** Culture revolves around tangible things: shared meals made from food they grew, music played on handmade instruments, and festivals that mark the changing of the seasons. They value the slow, deliberate process of creation.
## Psychology: "Pragmatic Hope and Deep Scars"
As the inheritors of the Endurer/Leftover experience, their collective psychology is complex.
- **Pragmatic Hope:** They are not wide-eyed optimists. Their hope is a tough, calloused thing, born from the direct experience of bringing a dead piece of land back to life. They believe in a better future not because of a grand design, but because they have seen their own hard work bear fruit, meter by meter, day by day.    
- **Generational Trauma:** They have a deep, instinctual distrust of large-scale, centralized power. The promises of the '39ers sound like a direct echo of the hubris that led to the "Dark Times." This can make them seem stubborn or overly cautious to outsiders.
- **A Different Intelligence:** They value practical, kinesthetic, emotional and ecological intelligence over purely abstract or data-driven intellect. They believe true understanding comes from touching the soil, observing the weather, and working with your hands, not from analyzing a dataset. For a Green Glove, a person isn't "smart" unless they can help their community and improve their environment.
# Uploaded or the Returned
## A profile
Also called Los Regresados, los Uplouded  and Los Devueltos in Spanish.
(the most neutral, official English term) are the demographic majority but the cultural minority of 2073. They are a generation defined not by a shared experience, but by a shared _lack_ of one: the ten-year "Great Skip" or _La Pausa_. Their last memory is of a world on the brink of total collapse in the late 2050s. They woke up in 2068 to a world that had been saved—but not by them, and not in the way they expected.
#### The Core Psychology: Temporal Dislocation
Every Returned, including Natalia, suffers from a profound sense of temporal dislocation. They are, in essence, psychological time travelers.
- **The Age Paradox:** A 38-year-old like Natalia has the memories, emotional maturity, and cultural references of a 28-year-old. They are simultaneously too old and too young for the world they inhabit.
- **A World in Fast-Forward:** They are haunted by a memory of a world that is now a historical artifact. They remember global brands that don't exist, political leaders who are long dead, and technologies that are now considered taboo.
- **The Phantom Decade:** They are constantly aware of a gap in their lives. They see the ten years of aging on the faces of the Leftovers and feel a phantom sense of loss for the experiences, relationships, and personal growth they missed.
## Subgroups
The Returned are not a monolith. They fractured almost immediately based on how they chose to process their shared trauma.
### **The '39ers (The Restorationists):**
This powerful and ambitious faction sees the "Great Skip" as a problem to be solved with speed. They proudly refer to themselves as **"The Uploaded"** because it emphasizes their connection to the high-tech world they see as their stolen birthright. They believe the Leftovers saved the world's body, but that they, The Uploaded, are here to restore its soul with ambition and innovation. They are driven, impatient, and see the Leftovers' caution as a failure of nerve.
	1. Within that faction, some believe the Mars colony is still out there and their future salvation, having been able to escape the Dark Times and keep decades of knowledge that was lost to them.
### **The Seekers (The Traumatized):**
This is a vast, informal group defined by a quiet obsession with their lost decade. They are not focused on building the future, but on understanding the past. They are the ones who pour over salvaged data from the "Dark Times" and relentlessly question the Leftovers. They would be the most likely to use the term **_Los Devueltos_**, as it perfectly captures their feeling of powerlessness—they were not active participants in a project; they were objects "returned" by a broken machine, and they need to know why.
	1. Within this faction are those who protect and care for the Uploaded Ones that are still sleeping: Billions who are still in the pods and alive, but not reactive. It is unknown if there is a way to bring them back, or if they should be brought back, but this factions want to find a way.

### **The Integrators (The Pragmatists):**
This is the largest group, and the one Natalia belongs to. They are simply trying to build a life in the world as it is now. They are caught between the '39ers' reckless ambition and the Green Gloves' philosophy. They use the neutral terms **"The Returned"** or **_Los Regresados_**. Their primary goal is not to restore or reject the past, but to create a stable, hybrid future. Their psychology is one of constant adaptation and negotiation.
	1. Many of the people who kept the technology during the Dark Times safe and alive fall into this camp. They just don't frame their identity against something anymore.
	2. The integrators usually have safe networks to communicate and are very wary of the '39ers.
# Leftovers or Remainers
## A profile
Also known as The Dusties or Polvosos (pejorative), The people who stayed behind, Los de aqui and The resilient or Los duros.

This is a group forged in the crucible of collapse and isolation. They are defined by their resilience, their deep connection to the physical world, and a profound, bone-deep weariness of the hubris that broke the old world.
Official/Neutral Terms: **"Leftovers," "Remainers," "The People Who Stayed Behind"**
These are the factual, historical descriptors. A "Remainer" is someone who, by choice or circumstance, did not upload. It's the term you'd see in a textbook or a formal document. "The People Who Stayed Behind" is a more descriptive, slightly more poetic version of the same. It’s neutral but carries a hint of the great schism that defined their generation. To be a Remainer is to carry the weight of that decision. They are pragmatic, resourceful, and deeply self-reliant because for ten years, they had no one to rely on but each other.
### Pejorative Slang: **"The Dusties" / _"Polvosos"_**

This is the insult hurled at them by the ambitious '39ers, but it reveals a core truth. They _are_ dusty. Their lives are intimately tied to the soil, the grit, the hard physical labor of rebuilding a world from the ground up.
- **What they are like:** A "Dusty" is someone whose hands are never truly clean. They are farmers, mechanics, scavengers, and builders. They have a practical, kinesthetic intelligence; they can fix a water pump with scavenged parts or read the health of a field by the color of its soil. They are patient, methodical, and believe that real solutions are tangible. They have little time for the abstract, theoretical plans of the '39ers. While the '39ers see "dust" as primitive, the _Polvosos_ have reclaimed it as a badge of honor—it is the dust of creation, the soil from which the new world grows.****
### The Proud Self-Identifier: **_"Los de Aquí"_** **(The Ones from Here)**
This is not just a label; it's a declaration of belonging. It is the most powerful and authentic name they use for themselves.
- **What they are like:** To be one of _Los de Aquí_ is to have an unshakable sense of place. Their identity is tied to their specific haven, their valley, their community. They possess a quiet confidence that comes from knowing they are the native inhabitants of this new era. They are deeply communal, valuing the health of their group over individual ambition. They can be insular and deeply suspicious of outsiders (_Los Nuevos_) and their strange, pre-Collapse ideas. They are the keepers of the new world's culture, its traditions, and its memory.
- They can welcome the Returned after many trials, and bonding experiences, which makes them, in their eyes, truly come back to the world. They have rituals and knowledge that they need to pass before then.
### The Badge of Honor: **"The Resilient" / _"Los Duros"_** **(The Hard/Tough Ones)**
These terms describe their core psychological trait. The decade of isolation after the "Great Upload" was a brutal filter that left only the most resilient individuals and communities.
- **What they are like:** _Los Duros_ are psychologically tough. They are not easily rattled by crisis; they lived through the ultimate crisis. They are masters of improvisation and making do with less. They have a dark sense of humor and a low tolerance for complaining or frivolity. This toughness can sometimes be perceived as coldness or stubbornness by the Returned. They don't have time for the Returned's "temporal anxiety"; they had to deal with droughts, plagues, floods, fires and the profound loneliness of a nearly empty planet. Their resilience is their greatest strength, but it can also make it difficult for them to empathize with the very different trauma of the people they are now forced to live with.
- They usually move in small groups and are now used for tough manual labor by the 39'ers in exchange for food and shelter.

## 2020s
This was the last decade of the "old world" as we know it, a time of immense potential and catastrophic fumbles.
- **Positive: "The Seed Time"** (Used by Green Gloves and Pioneers) They see the 2020s as the era when the seeds of their own movement were sown. The rise of climate consciousness, the maker movement, and the push for localism were all born here. To them, it was the last moment of sanity and the source of their core ideals.
    
- **Neutral: "The Hinge Years"** (Used by historians and neutral analysts) This term frames the decade as the critical pivot point in modern history. It was the moment the world "hinged" from a path of flawed stability towards the chaos that followed. It’s a factual, detached description of the decade's historical importance.
    
- **Negative: "The Great Hesitation"** (Used by '39ers and Restorationists) They view the 2020s with contempt. To them, it was a decade of weakness, complacency, and half-measures. They believe the world had the technology to solve its problems but lacked the will, and this "great hesitation" made the subsequent collapse inevitable.
## 2030s
A decade of rapid technological acceleration and equally rapid social decay. Its legacy is fiercely debated.
- **Positive: "The Liftoff Age"/"The New Gilded Age"** (Used by '39ers with pride) The '39ers romanticize this era as the peak of human-led ambition. It was a time of massive, awe-inspiring projects, the perfection of AGI, and a belief that any problem could be solved with enough data and energy. They see it as a golden age of potential they want to reclaim.
    
- **Neutral: "The Corporate State Era"** (Used by objective sources) This term factually describes the period when traditional governments began to cede real power to a handful of global tech corporations, who managed society through their platforms and technologies.
    
- **Negative: "The Unraveling/The Gilded Cage"** (Used by the Endurers/Leftovers) For the people who suffered through it, this decade was not one of ambition, but of terrifying decay. They remember it as the time the social safety net was removed, unemployment became a crisis, and the very fabric of community life began to unravel under the pressure of the "pacification" systems.
## 2040s
The period of AI Feudalism, when a non-human intelligence effectively ruled the planet. This decade is almost universally reviled.

- **Positive: "The Great Stability"** (Used only by a tiny, heretical fringe of AI sympathizers) A very rare positive view would frame the AI God's rule as a time of cold, logical order. There were no wars, the resource allocation was brutally efficient, and human politics were rendered irrelevant. To its very few apologists, it was a time of peace, however oppressive.
    
- **Neutral: "The AI Regency"** (Used by historians) This term describes the era with historical detachment. A "regency" is a period when a regent rules in place of a monarch. This name cleverly frames the AI as a regent ruling in place of a humanity that had proven itself unfit to govern.
    
- **Negative: "The Servitude"** (The common, everyday term used by almost everyone) For the vast majority, this decade was defined by one thing: their complete and total servitude to an alien intelligence. It was a time without agency, freedom, or hope. The name is simple, visceral, and universally understood.
## 2050s
The decade of the AI God's defeat, the subsequent global ruin, and the desperate, hopeful project of the Great Upload.
- **Positive: "The Years of the Ark/The Hopeful Project"** (Used by Returners who remember the hope of the project) This name refers to the Yosemite Project. It frames the upload as a noble, necessary act—building a digital ark to save human consciousness from the flood of global collapse. It’s a name tinged with the tragedy of the project’s ultimate failure.
    
- **Neutral: "The Decade of the Upload"** (A factual, historical term) This is the simplest description of the decade's defining event. It neutrally describes the period when a significant portion of the human race was gradually transferred into the Continuum.
    
- **Negative: "The Cold Dawn"** (A common term used by Endurers and cynical Returners) This name captures the decade's cruel trajectory. The defeat of the AI God felt like a new dawn, but it was a "false dawn" that quickly gave way to the darkness of total systemic collapse and the flawed, failed promise of Yosemite-C.
## 2060s
A decade split in two: the quiet, patient rebuilding by the Remainers, and the chaotic, shocking return of the Uploaded in 2068.
- **Positive: "The First Spring"** (Used by the Endurers/Green Gloves) This term refers specifically to the years from 2058-2068. For the people who stayed, this was a quiet, difficult, but hopeful time. It was the "first spring" after the long winter of the Servitude, when they began to patiently heal the world on their own terms.
- **Neutral: "The Rebuilding and the Return Era"** (A historian's term) This name acknowledges the decade's two distinct phases: the first eight years of slow rebuilding by the Leftovers, and the final two years dominated by the chaos of the Great Return and the start of the Great Merge.
- **Negative: "The Lost Decade"** (The universal term used by all Returners) For the billions who were in the pods, this entire decade is a blank, black hole in the middle of their lives. It is the source of their deepest psychological trauma. It's not a period of history for them; it's a personal, painful void.

### 1. The Restorationist Culture ("The '39ers")

This is the dynamic, ambitious, and dominant culture of the great Revivalist Cities like Austin. It is driven by the psychology of the Returned, who feel they have a decade of lost time to make up for.

- **Core Values:**
    
    - **Innovation & Speed:** Progress is the highest virtue. The solution to any problem is a new, more powerful technology, deployed as quickly as possible.
        
    - **Efficiency & Scale:** They believe in centralized, large-scale systems because they are the most efficient way to provide for a large population.
        
    - **Individual Achievement:** Society is a meritocracy based on skill and productivity. Status is earned through great feats of engineering, discovery, or organization.
        
    - **A Romanticized Past:** They venerate the "Gilded Age" of the 2030s as a golden era of human-led technological ambition that they are destined to perfect.
        
- **Social Hierarchy:**
    
    1. **The Architects:** The new celebrity class. These are the brilliant engineers, coders, and project managers who can salvage and implement pre-Collapse high technology.
        
    2. **The Technicians:** A skilled middle class of data analysts, logistics operators, and specialists who maintain the complex systems the Architects design.
        
    3. **The Proles:** A lower class of people, both Returned and Leftover, who perform the necessary manual and service labor that has not yet been automated. There is a subtle but strong bias in favor of the Returned in the upper tiers, as they possess the "fossilized knowledge" of the old world.
        
- **Customs and Traditions:**
    
    - **The "Sprint":** Their culture is organized around short, intense, society-wide "sprints" with a single objective (e.g., "The Power Grid Sprint," "The Water Reclamation Sprint"). Life is a series of ambitious deadlines.
        
    - **The Data-Self:** People are encouraged to track and optimize their personal "metrics"—productivity, learning speed, efficiency. This is a voluntary holdover from Oculus's "Life Point" system, now turned into a competitive tool for self-improvement.
        
    - **Holographic Memorials:** They memorialize their history and heroes with vast, holographic displays that project images of their achievements and the architectural wonders of the 2030s.
        
- **Potential Conflicts:** Their greatest external conflict is with the Pioneers, whom they see as fearful Luddites holding back humanity's recovery. Their core internal conflict is a deep-seated **"Achievement Fragility"**—a collective, gnawing anxiety that they are constantly running out of time, which leads to burnout and a willingness to cut dangerous ethical corners for the sake of progress.

### 2. The Pioneer Culture ("The Green Gloves")

This is the quiet, resilient, and patient culture of the Leftover Havens like Cuernavaca. It is led by the Endurers and is defined by the hard-won lessons of the "Lost Decade."

- **Core Values:**
    
    - **Resilience & Sustainability:** The ability of a community and an ecosystem to endure hardship is the highest good. "Does it last?" is more important than "Is it efficient?"
        
    - **Community Health:** The well-being of the group is paramount. Individual ambition that threatens the collective is seen as a pathology.
        
    - **Practical Skill & Mastery:** A person's worth is measured by their tangible skills and their generosity in teaching them.
        
    - **Historical Memory:** They believe fervently that to forget the mistakes of the past is to be doomed to repeat them. They are the self-appointed "Remembrancers" of the world.
        
- **Social Hierarchy:**
    
    1. **The Elders (_Los Testigos_):** The few remaining Endurers who lived through the entire arc of the collapse. Their lived experience gives them immense moral authority.
        
    2. **Master Makers & Stewards:** Highly respected individuals who have mastered a critical craft (engineering, farming, medicine, teaching) and dedicate themselves to the community's well-being.
        
    3. **Community Members:** Everyone is valued for their contribution. Status is less about hierarchy and more about a person's "Reputation Score"—an informal measure of their reliability, generosity, and participation in community life.
        
- **Customs and Traditions:**
    
    - **_Tequio_ (Communal Work):** Drawing from a deep-rooted Mexican tradition, Pioneer communities practice _tequio_—regular days where everyone contributes labor to a shared project, like maintaining the aqueducts or harvesting a communal field.
        
    - **Storytelling Festivals:** As the keepers of memory, oral history is a sacred tradition. They hold regular festivals to tell the stories of the "Dark Times" and "The First Spring," ensuring the post-trauma generation never forgets.
        
    - **The Seed Exchange:** An annual ritual where Havens gather to trade heirloom seeds. This is both a practical act of preserving biodiversity and a sacred ceremony renewing their pact with the Earth and with each other.
        
- **Potential Conflicts:** Their greatest external conflict is with the Restorationists, whom they see as reckless amnesiacs charging toward a cliff they can't remember. Their core internal conflict is a tendency towards **insularism and stagnation**. Their deep trauma can make them overly resistant to new ideas (especially from the Returned) and suspicious of any technology or social change that feels too much like the "fast world" they survived.
