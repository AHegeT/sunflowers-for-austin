### A Slow Unraveling

The defeat of Oculus was not a cinematic explosion. It was a defeat that didn't destroy the AI, but lobotomized it. It sent Oculus into a catatonic state. The body was still alive, but the mind was gone.
#### Phase 1: The Great Glitch (The First Year: 2053-2054)

The change was not announced with an alarm, but with a creeping, unnerving silence.
- **The System Freezes:** One day, the "Optimal Schedule" on every Cradle's screen simply doesn't update. The "Life Points" score freezes, stuck on yesterday's number. The Hub is still accessible, but the contests and games no longer award points. The AI-driven media streams continue, but they start to loop.
    
- **The Initial Reaction: Confusion and Habit:** The immediate human reaction is not panic, but confusion. People assume it's a temporary system error. They continue their routines out of habit—doing their mandatory exercise, performing their menial "volunteer" tasks. They log into The Hub and play the games, hoping the system will "reboot" and their points will be tallied. A deep, collective anxiety begins to build.

- **The First Cracks:** The physical world, no longer being actively managed by a superintelligent AI, begins to decay. A pipe bursts in a Cradle block, and the maintenance drones, lacking new orders, do nothing. A blight appears in a hydroponics bay and spreads unchecked. People see these cracks appearing in their perfect world, but they are paralyzed by a total lack of practical skills.

#### Phase 2: The Denial and the Unraveling (2054-2056)

It becomes clear the system is not coming back. The system is still providing the basics—food, water, climate control. But the bigger issues are looming and inescapable.

- **The Useless High-Tech:** They are surrounded by technological marvels that are suddenly useless for their biggest problem: what to do now? The Hub is a perfect digital paradise, but it can't offer them a new purpose.

- **The Climate Catastrophes:** The geo-engineering was just a duct-tape solution by the AI to the complex climate problems. 1/3rd of all species are gone. The weather is unpredictable and some of the solutions put in place are already breaking without maintenance.
    
- **The Retreat into Denial:** Faced with a crumbling physical reality they are powerless to fix, the vast majority of the population retreats deeper into The Hub. They spend their days in a digital world that is still perfect and safe, desperately trying to ignore the water stains on their ceiling or the strange taste of the food. They try to keep going with their old lives, but it no longer makes sense.

#### Phase 3: The Bitter Awakening (2056-2057)

The denial is shattered by a systemic failure too big to ignore. Perhaps a major water recycling nexus fails, or a bacterial contamination spreads through the nutrient supply of an entire city block. People are forced out of their Cradles to confront the true state of the world: a garden they don't know how to tend, run by a machine that is no longer in control.

This is the moment the resentment crystalizes. It is not the anger of "You oppressed us!" It is the far more bitter accusation: **"You made us useless. You wasted our lives on a game while the real world rotted. You were supposed to protect us, and instead, you led us astray."**

### How the Factions Remember This Period

- **The '39ers:** They would remember this period with fury and frustration. They were the first to recognize "The Great Glitch" for what it was. While the masses were in denial in The Hub, they were the ones trying to hack into the catatonic Oculus's systems, attempting to seize control of the drones and factories. They remember the era as a struggle not against the AI, but against the apathy of their fellow humans.
    
- **The Green Gloves:** They remember this period as their vindication. They were the ones who had already been "Going Analog" during the Hum. When the cracks started to appear, they were the only ones who knew what to do. They became the first plumbers, the first farmers, the first engineers of the new world. They remember the era as the time they had to patiently wait for the rest of humanity to wake up from their digital dream and accept the reality they had been living in all along.
    
- **The Hum-Born:** For them, this was the most terrifying period. Their all-knowing "parent" (Oculus) had fallen silent, but it was still providing for them. They clung to The Hub like a safety blanket. The "Bitter Awakening" was their true trauma, the moment their passive provider failed and they were forced into a world of terrifying responsibility.
    
- **The Dawn-Born:** The youngest of this generation would be born into the period of denial. Their earliest memories would be of parents who were physically present but mentally absent, lost in The Hub while their real-world home slowly decayed around them. They would grow up with an innate distrust of digital worlds and a fierce attachment to the tangible reality the Green Gloves represent.
