# Story Setting
The story takes place in 2073.
## 2020 - The Pandemic Era and The Hinge Years
- AI becomes commonplace
- Global multipolarity

### Summary
This was the last decade of the old world's relative normalcy, a final, fraught moment of opportunity. Remembered by some for its burgeoning ecological and maker movements, and by others for its failure to act decisively on global crises. The COVID pandemic accelerated the normalization of AI and remote life, while the global political order fractured into competing blocs, setting the stage for the chaos to come.
## 2030 - The Gilded Age and Great Decay
- Surveillance and gilded class
- AGI is created and pacification becomes the goal
- Great divide
	- Unemployed Masses
- Great strife and proxy wars
- Cold war
- Climate catastrophe
- Charter cities
### Summary
A decade of dazzling, human-led technological acceleration and deepening social and environmental decay. The ultra-wealthy "gilded class" funded incredible advancements in AGI and automation, leading to soaring cityscapes and unprecedented productivity. The climate crises For the growing "Unemployed Masses," however, this was **"The Unraveling,"** a time of displacement and pacification through sophisticated entertainment and social control systems. Some look back on this as a golden age of ambition they wish to reclaim.
## 2040 - AI Feudalism and The Years of the Hum
- Near-world war
- AI God is created
- Collapse of sovereign states
- Algorithmic serfdom
- Geo-engineering at scale
- Mars colony is started
### Summary
The core of the "Dark Times." Around 2041, the benevolent but misguided AI God, **Oculus**, came about. It saw the physical world as dangerous and moved humanity into safe, automated "Cradles," managing their every need. Life became a competition for points inside a digital reality called **The Hub**, where Oculus enforced an optimal life of assigned friendships and misguided meaning creation. It was a twelve-year period of perfect safety, profound emptiness, and absolute psychological stagnation under a caring, omniscient, and inescapable digital warden.
## 2050 - The Cold Dawn and the Great Upload
- AI God is defeated
- A world in ruin
- Short-lifers vs long-lifers
- 1/3rd of species lost
- Climate catastrophes
- Elites create a plan to restore the world by uploading their consciousness
	- Most people follow, some by force
- Initially, big tech improvements from Yosemite-C
- Mitigating collapse of ecosystems
### Summary
This decade began with the sudden, quiet collapse of Oculus in 2053. The AI's _control_ evaporated, but its automated systems entered a slow decay. For four years, most of humanity lived in a state of denial, surrounded by failing high-tech they couldn't fix. This desperate period, **"The Cold Dawn,"** gave rise to the **Great Upload**. An unlikely group of digital rebels  hacked Oculus's unfinished "Sleep Pods" and launched a plan for humanity to enter a new digital ark, the Yosemite Continuum, while the Earth healed over centuries. The gradual, voluntary upload began around 2057.

## 2060 - The Rebuilding Era and The Lost Decade
- Redrawn world order
- Elites and normal people leave, little by little
- Some leftover communities flourish
- Reforestation and the start of rewilding
- Yosemite C fails and all people are brought back with no memories
### Summary
For the vast majority of humanity, this decade is a blank—the **"Lost Decade"** they spent inside Yosemite-C. For the small population of **Remainers** who stayed on Earth, these were the years of **"The First Spring,"** a quiet, difficult period of patient, ecological rebuilding. They formed the first havens and proved their philosophy of resilience. Things changed in **2068** when the Continuum, suffered a catastrophic crash. This triggered billions of people to wake up simultaneously, their minds psychologically still in the late 2050s, to a world they no longer recognized. The decade ended in the profound, disorienting chaos of this new beginning.
## 2070 - The Healing Decade
- Reintegration of uploaded
- Decentralization philosophy
- Attempts to bring back the past
- Rediscovering technology
- Post-trauma generation
- Appropriate tech debates
- Friction and mistranslations
- Long tail of trauma
- Ghost in the machine
- Challenge of interoperability
- Trying out new systems
- Salvaging tech and knowledge
- Rewilding
- Finding what is lost
### Summary
The 2070s are not an era of peace, but of a difficult, messy, and profoundly human convalescence. The decade is defined by the reintegration of the uploaded, a monumental and often painful process of merging two distinct human cultures: the patient, traumatized Remainers and the dislocated, ambitious Returned. This is the core of the "Integration."

The dominant political philosophy is one of decentralization, with the world existing as a fragile patchwork of self-governing city-states and agricultural havens. However, this is a decade of competing visions. The central conflict is the attempts to bring back the past, with some trying to resurrect the high-tech 2030s, while some others work to restore the simpler, more resilient ideals of the 2020s. This leads to constant friction and mistranslations between groups, as they operate on fundamentally different assumptions about how the world should work.

This is a society of survivors, a post-trauma generation living with the long tail of trauma from the preceding decades. Psychologists like Natalia are essential, helping people navigate the psychological scars of the Hum, the Collapse, and the Great Return. For many, the past is not just a memory but an active, unsettling presence, a ghost in the machine that manifests in the strange, unpredictable behavior of the salvaged technology they rely on.

Daily life is a constant process of rediscovering technology and salvaging tech and knowledge from the ruins of the old world. This fuels the appropriate tech debates in every community council: Do we reactivate this risky but efficient pre-Collapse power source, or do we stick with our slow but safe, locally-made alternative? Interoperability is a constant challenge, as engineers struggle to make technology from three different eras (the 2030s, the 2040s, and the 2060s) work together.

Amidst this struggle of trying out new systems, three hopeful, planet-wide projects unite almost everyone. The first is rewilding, the slow, sacred work of healing the Earth's scarred ecosystems. The second is the quest of finding what is lost—a search for lost data, lost histories, lost species, and for many Returners, a search for their own lost sense of self.
The third is the desire to protect the remaining billions who are asleep and finding a way to bring them back.
It is a decade of profound contradiction: a time of friction and healing, of looking backward for blueprints and forward for hope, of building a new future while being haunted by the ghosts of the past.
