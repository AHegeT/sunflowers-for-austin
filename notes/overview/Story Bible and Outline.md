# Overview

- **Tone and Feel:**
	- Solar punk. A blend of hopeful, community-focused futurism with a deep undercurrent of existential loss. The aesthetic emphasizes nature integrated with artisanal technology, while the mood grapples with what gives life meaning after the dream of digital immortality was erased and replaced by corporate consumerism.
	- Melancholic Hope
		- The **melancholy** comes from the profound and pervasive sense of loss that defines the world of 2073. It is the personal loss of the Returned, who are haunted by their ten-year "Lost Decade." It is the collective loss of a planet scarred by ecological and technological catastrophe. And it is the historical loss of knowledge, culture, and a future that was stolen by the hubris and carelessness of the past.
		- The **hope** is not bright or naive; it is a tough, pragmatic, and hard-won thing. It is visible in the patient work of the Green Gloves, in the green shoots of the rewilding zones, and in the sheer, stubborn resilience of communities trying to build a better, more thoughtful world from the ruins. The overall feel is quiet and introspective, with a constant, low-grade tension humming beneath a surface of purposeful work. It is a world that is holding its breath, knowing that its fragile peace could be shattered at any moment.
	- Dialogue: Lots of it. Witty, banter-heavy, and defensive. Characters use humor to hide pain. There are many emotions underneath.
	- Authors for inspiration:
		- Becky Chambers: Mundane intimacy of sci-fi. Emphasis on character dynamics and emotional bonding.
		- Philip Pullman (_His Dark Materials_): Compakins as externalized souls of sorts.
		- William Gibson (Neuromancer): A greener version.
		- Jeff VanderMeer (Annihilation): A flavor of "New Weird" or ecological horror.
		- Hayao Miyazaki (Studio Ghibli): Tactile technology with nature. Antagonist to ally arcs after the protagonists prove themselves.
    
- **Core Question:**
	- How can we move forward? What can we learn from others?
	- Can a found family heal the wounds left by a lost one, and how do you build a future when the past is a mystery you can't even remember?
	- In a world shattered by its own technological ambition, what is the true path to recovery?
    
- **The Hook:**
	- An ex-psychologist, a synthetic creature and an optimistic teenage student team up to unravel the mystery of the missing 10 years of the world and to find the path to heal a broken world.
	- In a future where billions of "Returned" who skipped the last decade are rebuilding society alongside the few who remember the apocalypse, a Returner psychologist is summoned to investigate the mysterious death of her mentor. Her only clue is a living, psychoactive, Aztec-style codex that only she can awaken—an artifact that holds the key to his murder and a dangerous truth that could either heal their fractured world or plunge it back into a second dark age.

- **Themes:**
	- Found family
	- Understanding and mistranslations
	- Betrayal and forgiveness
	- Breaking and fixing
	- Solar-powered regeneration

# Setting

- **Magic System / Technology:** 
	- [[The Codex]]
	- **Compakins:** The core technology is a new form of engineered life called Compakins, which are a composite of machine and human spirit. They are designed for a mutualistic relationship, bonding with a human to better understand the world.
    
	- **The Bonding Process:** Compakins begin in an "egg" form. They require a nurturing period of approximately two weeks where the human must hold, warm, and speak to the egg. This is not a gimmick; the connection is critical for the Compakin's proper neurological and processing development. During this time, the egg's haptic sensors collect biometric data and unintended DNA traces from the human's hands, which personalizes its cognitive architecture.
	    
	- **Haliatus (The "Life Breath"):** The bond is finalized when the human performs the "Haliatus" ritual, breathing their "spirit" or psyche data into the egg's Haliatus Port. The egg then "hatches".
	    
	- **Evolution of Purpose:** Originally, Compakins were sacred, serving as living archives to preserve the nuances of human consciousness after a great cataclysm. The bonding was a solemn ritual. In the current era, they have been commercialized and are marketed as personalized companions or advanced pets, with the bonding process streamlined and commodified.
    
- **Cultures & Societies:** The world's cultures are not black and white, but are instead different responses to the collective trauma of the "Great Reset", different experiments on how to move forward.
	- **Cuernavaca (The Seed):** This city is not a solar punk haven but rather the _seed_ of one. Much like Paloma's parents, who returned from the Continuum emotionally distant and "weird," the city is in a state of quiet shock. Having been "left behind" by the digital boom, it now feels hollowed out and lacks a clear direction. Its potential for a vibrant, community-focused future exists, but it is nascent and must be actively cultivated by the protagonists, who will bring "teachings" from other cultures to help it heal and grow.
    
	- **Austin (The Aimless Engine):** Austin is a powerful society stuck in an identity crisis. It invested its entire identity in the promise of digital immortality, and the collapse of the Yosemite Continuum left it with immense, concentrated corporate power but no purpose or dream. It is "stuck in a loop," pursuing mass-produced consumerism as a hollow replacement for its lost identity. While it can appear soulless, it is not without its own lessons and value; its advanced systems and knowledge could be a force for good if they were "unblocked" and given a new, meaningful direction.
	- [[Different Factions]]
		- Revival Culture ("The '39ers")
			- Defined by a restless ambition to reclaim their "Lost Decade," Restorationist culture is a fast-paced meritocracy that values **speed, scale, and technological solutions** above all else. Centered in bustling Revivalist Cities like Austin, these '39ers venerate the pre-Collapse 2030s as a golden age of human achievement they are meant to perfect. Their society is dynamic and innovative but is also haunted by a collective anxiety.
		- Pioneer Culture ("The Green Gloves")
			- Rooted in the hard-won wisdom of the "Lost Decade," Pioneer culture values **patience, community health, and ecological resilience**. Organized in decentralized, self-sufficient Havens like Cuernavaca, they prioritize practical, repairable technology and the slow work of healing the planet. Their society is stable and deeply connected to the land and each other.

			- They want to bring back the AI God. Some of them believe this time it can be controlled, some that it will make things right.

## Phenomena & Mechanics
### Temporal Dislocation Syndrome (TDS)

- The Experience: A dissociation where the consciousness slips off the "track" of the present, often triggered by intense emotional recall or trauma.

- The Visual: The subject perceives a "Red Landscape", a desolate, timeless version of reality (possibly the Earth as it would be without life, the future, or a distortion of the Continuum).

- Anchoring: The sufferer must find a visual "Anchor" in the landscape and mentally "scrub" the timeline forward or backward to find a resonant memory to latch onto.

- Somatic Override: A therapy technique from Austin involving rhythmic tapping (vagus nerve stimulation) to force the parasympathetic nervous system to reset, often used to prevent TDS episodes.
	- **The Cost:** While effective in the short term, chronic use leads to **Emotional Fragmentation**. The suppressed emotions do not disappear; they accumulate in the subconscious, often leading to sudden, catastrophic "system failures" (TDS episodes) or requiring a "venting" mechanism (like a Compakin).
	- **In the Story:** Natalia uses this reflexively, treating her body like hardware. It is a symbol of her Austin conditioning—the belief that pain is just an inefficiency to be debugged.

Compakin Bond Mechanics:

- The Tether: Compakins act as a stabilizing "weight" for human consciousness. Because they are designed to interface with both "Real Time" and "Continuum Time," they can physically ground a human experiencing a TDS episode. This is not initially known.

- Emotional Venting: Young Compakins (Stage 1) lack the capacity to filter their bonded human's emotions. If the human suppresses too much (like with Somatic Override), the Compakin may suffer a "Feedback Loop," venting the energy as light or raw data, which can inadvertently trigger the TDS they are meant to prevent.

## Core Plot Beats

_(This is a high-level, four-point summary of your plot.)_

1. **The Beginning:** The setup of the world and characters in their normal life, ending with the **Inciting Incident** that kicks off the story.
    
2. **The Midpoint:** A major turning point where the character moves from reacting to proactively driving the plot. Often includes a major revelation or reversal of fortune.
    
3. **The Point of No Return:** The moment before the climax where the character makes their final choice and commits to the final confrontation. All other options are gone.
    
4. **The End:** The story's climax and the resolution that follows.
    

## Narrative Structure

[[Chapters Outline]]

### Intro

- **Prologue (Optional):** A short scene that establishes the tone, hints at "The Secret," or shows a key past event from a unique perspective.
    
- **Early Chapters:** Introduce the "Narrative Spine" character and their world. Show what their normal life is like before the Inciting Incident disrupts it.
    

### Part 1: The Setup

The character reacts to the Inciting Incident and begins their journey. They are learning the new rules of their situation and are often on the defensive.

### Part 2: The Confrontation

Following the Midpoint, the character now has a clearer understanding of the stakes and the antagonist. They begin making their own plans and taking the fight to the enemy, leading to bigger successes and bigger failures.

### Part 3: The Resolution

Following the Point of No Return, the character enacts their final plan. This part contains the Climax.

### Climax

The final confrontation where the protagonist faces the core conflict head-on. The story's central question is answered.
