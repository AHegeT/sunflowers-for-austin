# Cleaned Transcript

### Speaker 1

On _Sunflowers for Austin_—this is Chapter 5, the part where they visit the sleep pods.

  

They meet two researchers from the same company who have been pitted against each other. The company is Apex Technologies, and Natalia uses her past connections there to gain access.

  

The two researchers represent two competing factions within the company. One research team wants to recreate the Yosemite Continuum from scratch using prototypes and early research. The other team simply wants to re-upload consciousness using the existing pods, arguing that it's far easier since the hardware is already functional and was figured out once before. The two groups are completely opposed; each mocks the other's approach as foolish, and they rarely interact under normal circumstances.

  

They are brought together to answer questions from the visiting children.

  

One of the kids asks, "Are you able to communicate with the people inside the pods?"

  

One researcher scoffingly dismisses it: "That's stupid—of course not, they're asleep."

  

The second researcher counters: "Well, it's more complicated than that. We are able to send messages, and there is a noticeable shift in their wave response. They seem to send something back, though we can't interpret it—it looks like gibberish."

  

The first researcher argues back: "What are you talking about? That's ridiculous."

  

They explain that the Yosemite Continuum is not a man-made digital network in the traditional sense, but rather an existing range of wavelengths—a specific frequency band. All the data and consciousness streams are densely compressed within that frequency. Because the medium has evolved into frequencies far more refined than what early sensors were designed to handle, standard tools can no longer interpret the signal, even though the data is clearly active and the Continuum is still running.

  

The kids understand this concept through their haptic gloves. One student mentions having the gloves, but the researcher dismisses it patronizingly: "Oh, nice toy—keep experimenting and learning about science!"

  

In reality, it hints that the students could use their haptic gloves to interface with the sleeping pods. The gloves emit specific frequencies and signals, and while a single signal is unidirectional, coordinating multiple channels could establish two-way communication.

  

The researchers continue bickering, and the students move on.

  

These researchers worked on the original Yosemite Continuum project, one of many corporate-funded initiatives worldwide. However, they were eventually removed from the project for not being smart enough. Both remain paralyzed by intellectual insecurity, obsessing over proving who is more qualified rather than making real scientific progress.

  

They also note that the shutdown of the Yosemite Continuum was not a random global accident. Telemetry shows two distinct download pulses occurring roughly 0.5 milliseconds apart. That tiny gap proves it was an intentional, phased event rather than an indiscriminate systems failure.

  

This sets up a major plot revelation: Arcturus initially purged and downloaded the "less intelligent" populace because the Continuum was facing resource and compute constraints. Leon refused to accept that hierarchy. To give the people on Earth a fighting chance and exact retribution, Leon triggered the second phase, forcibly downloading the top percentage of elites, corrupting their data so they had no way back, and dooming them to what they considered "hell on Earth".

  

Leon's second sin was knowingly hijacking and running the process on a compute network that belonged to Oculus, aware that dormant seeds were scattered across it that would eventually accumulate power and hatch into a new Oculus. Despite the danger, Leon used it anyway.
