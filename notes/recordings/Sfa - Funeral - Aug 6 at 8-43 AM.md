### Speaker 1

Okay, _Sunflowers for Austin_—let's talk about Julia's speech during the funeral.

  

People should be saying horrible things about Leon. Someone says, "I only came here so I can spit wherever he ends up buried." Another person claims, "He's the reason the world is ruined, and why we don't stand a chance—why everything is going to hell." Someone else calls him a selfish person who corrupted the Yosemite project into something awful.

  

Everyone is not merely condemning his character; they are actively spiteful—they truly hate him.

  

Julia responds to this atmosphere. She says: "It's a shame. Funerals should happen in life, not in death. We should learn what we meant to others, and if we hurt them, we should be given the chance to make it up to them—what a waste that he never gets that opportunity.

  

"If funerals happened in life, people would actually have the guts to say what they really feel. The most craven wouldn't show up at all, because they prefer to hoard their resentment like a treasure, and speaking at a funeral gives that away. Telling it directly to someone gives it away. But the people who actually matter would get a chance to be honest and share how they truly feel. And perhaps even the worst offender or sinner would get to hear the one kind act they did, even if it was done by mistake."

  

Looking around at the room, Julia adds: "They're going to wear themselves out. They really should save all of this venom for my funeral—they'll have a blast then."

  

Then she turns to Natalia and tells her that if she has any doubts, or if anything inside her saw a different side to Leon—if he ever showed her a single kindness—she should bring it forward. "Because even the worst sinner deserves a kind word at the end of their story."

  

Then Julia mutters, "Well... maybe not the worst," quietly referring to herself.

  

That sets up the thematic arc for Natalia in this chapter. Natalia's biggest flaw is going with the flow and assuming there must be valid truth to everyone else's opinions. She keeps wondering, Why do they think this? Was he really that bad? What am I missing? Through this confrontation, she realizes she needs to connect with her own feelings, even if she can't fully articulate them yet.

  

When she gives her tribute and sings her song, she hears the crowd booing and complaining—_"Why is she singing? This is awful!"_ But Natalia closes her eyes, tunes them out, and connects directly with her own grief and authentic feelings. That is her character arc in this chapter.
