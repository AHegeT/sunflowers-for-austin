This is for _Sunflowers for Austin_—the scene where Cody transforms.

  

Natalia finds him the next morning. He is basking in the sun, closing his eyes and enjoying the warmth. When he hears her approach, he says, "The sun is nice, isn't it?"

  

He also cracks a joke and gestures with his paws.

  

Natalia is surprised and asks, "How do you know how to use so many words?"

  

Cody replies, "Oh, I'm connected to your t-box, so I can speak any language you know." He essentially has access to the t-box translation interface, allowing him to learn and speak. He actually enjoys having that structure; he doesn't experience it as a limitation, or at least not an unwelcome one.

  

There is also this concept regarding the founding compakin, where everything seemed like a wonderful day, which originally convinced Arcturus to hold off and wait.

  

The underlying problem is that when computations wrap around themselves, they can't actually fully resolve. In the worst case, that loop eventually leads to oblivion; in the best case, it simply stagnates. They don't have the definitive answer yet. Even as they foresee how circumstances are going to change, they cannot afford to detach from the present state of the world—they have to live through reality as it unfolds.
