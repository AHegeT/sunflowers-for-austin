# Chapter 2 — current-draft review

Current decisions: [Story Decisions](Story%20Decisions.md). The conflicts below record the initial review; confirmed name, appearance, missing-decade, and Monty timeline corrections have since been applied. Structural questions remain for the chapter edit.

Scope: the repository reading draft, its planning notes, the student roster, and relevant Chapter 3 continuity. Google Docs have not been compared. This review does not change the manuscript or establish a new canon.

## What the current chapter contains

1. **The Fugue:** After hearing that Leon died, Natalia loses track of her journey and time, is found by Monty, and withdraws into Lina’s house. She resents Leon and minimizes their relationship. Somatic override lets her suppress the grief and resume functioning.
2. **Back in Action:** She discovers it is already her first teaching day. Leon wanted her and Monty to cover his classes. She expected psychology, learns it is history, and rapidly memorizes his disordered notes.
3. **Arrival and principal:** Marymount’s old buildings, largely Returner student population, and familiar teaching expectations contrast with Cuernavaca outside. Waiting with Monty exposes the difference between his fifteen years of separation and her five experienced years. Mendez questions her qualifications, then sends her to class by a needlessly long route.
4. **First class:** She uses override again and delivers a precise, detached lecture about pre-fall economics and the 2030s. She later tells Monty the class applauded and believes this means success.
5. **The Hacienda:** Monty reveals Leon’s concealed laboratory room and the mycelium mechanism, explains his misleading report of where he found the body, and describes a prepared ritual death. Natalia recognizes the connection to her conch/book. They leave with an uneasy sense of being watched.
6. **Second and third classes:** Without fully suppressing her reactions, she notices tapping, private jokes, and increasingly exaggerated applause. Paloma is isolated and addressed as “miss prefect.” When challenged, Paloma argues for doing useful work rather than hearing another history lecture. Natalia realizes the applause was mockery, dismisses the class, and flees in shame.
7. **The stairs:** Paloma apologizes: she hoped joining the ridicule would stop the others picking on her. Natalia begins to listen rather than perform the teacher role. The chapter ends with an invitation to try something tomorrow, without specifying the activity or showing its outcome.

The central tension is already present: Natalia can control delivery and mute her distress, but those tools do not let her understand an unfamiliar classroom. The ending opens a relationship rather than resolving the classroom problem.

## Plans and companion material are different versions, not automatically errors

[Chapter 2 plan](../story/chapters/Chapter%202.md) has failure on the first day, a changed approach, Paloma testing Natalia, then the Hacienda and a principal confrontation. The current manuscript instead develops the failure over three classes and visits the Hacienda before the collapse. Treat that as a structural revision to choose between, not proof that the reading draft is wrong.

[Setting plan](../story/chapters/Exploring%20the%20Setting%20for%20Chapter%202.md) describes collaborative Remainer learning alongside the rigid school model. Marymount may intentionally be a Returner-oriented exception. A short concrete contrast could make Paloma’s demand for useful work more legible.

[Chapter 2.2 manuscript](../../manuscript/en/02.2-social_reengineering.md) already contains the classroom response: seating experiments, group projects, students sharing upload/family experiences, a more cooperative class, and Mendez putting Natalia on probation with extra subjects. It is not currently linked in SUMMARY. Decide which outcomes the main reading path requires; detailed experimental steps can remain a companion chapter.

The roster’s five Returners among eight named students does not strictly contradict the school’s roughly 80% Returner population: one cohort need not match a school-wide proportion. The words “only other Returner” in 2.2 at line 81 have an ambiguous referent; identify whether “her” means Natalia or Paloma. The roster marks Paloma a Remainer.

## Age, date, and identity reconciliation

- **Natalia:** [main brief](../characters/Character%20Briefs%20-%20Main.md) at line 27 says a five-year memory gap; lines 83–87 describe a missing decade. Chapter 2’s “five years for me” at 148 can be consistent with fifteen elapsed years minus ten missing years, and should not be changed merely for using five. Her brief labels 2058–2068 age 21–32 despite a 2036 birth year; the first endpoint would be 22.
- **Monty:** Chapter 2 at 146 says fifteen years since he saw Natalia; his brief at 359 and 369 says thirteen years, with a last meeting in 2060. With a 2073 present, fifteen years would instead point to about 2058. His 2035 birth year makes 2035–2041 ages 0–6, not 0–8, and 2053–2060 ages 18–25, not 18–22.
- **Aurelius / Julia:** Julia’s brief gives Aurelius’s birth when she was 47 (2023), illness at 17 in 2040, but also death when she was 64 (2040). Arcturus’s brief says death at 25, which would point to about 2048. Illness onset versus death needs separation.
- **Mendez:** Age 43 and upload age 31 are not inherently contradictory. With 2073 as the present, a birth around 2030 and upload around 2061 can fit, allowing seven uploaded years and five years since 2068. The upload date is not yet fixed; the earlier source-note warning was too broad.
- **Monty’s name:** [Chapter 3](../../manuscript/en/03-the_egg.md) at 37 calls him **Raul Montante**; his brief at 339 calls him **Baltazar Montante**. The Chapter 1 planning note also retains **Matias**, likely an older draft name.
- **Principal’s surname:** Chapter 2 at 206 establishes **Lomeli Mendez**; Chapter 3 at 130 uses **Principal Lopez**. Determine whether this is a stale name or a different official.
- **Nicolai / Nikolai:** Both spellings appear in the Chapter 6 working note; the main brief uses Sebastian Nicolai. Some uses may refer to a family surname shared with Jan, rather than merely a nickname.
- **Lena:** Pretova in the brief and Chapter 4 at 115; Petrova in Chapter 4 at 299.
- **Paloma’s hair:** Curly brown in Chapter 2 at 445; curly black in her brief at 122 and the profile expanded from that brief.
- **Apex:** Apex Technologies in the manuscript; Apex Galilei in the main brief; Apex Gallilei in the secondary brief. These might be branding/subsidiary distinctions, but no distinction is currently established.
- **Codi / Cody; Green Hands / Green Gloves:** Variants appear across notes and manuscript. Decide whether these are spelling/translation variants or distinct deliberate terms.
- **World chronology:** First Spring’s boundary varies around 2057–2058. Older timeline summaries say everyone returned simultaneously in 2068; the manuscript establishes remaining sleepers and Chapter 5 describes two return waves. Do not silently discard the current mystery to match the older summary.

## Leon’s death: protect the difference between belief and truth

Chapters 1–3 largely support Natalia and Monty’s belief in a prepared suicide; Chapter 4 at 443 gives Natalia’s conclusion that Leon did not kill himself. The author brief contains a further planned explanation. These are different knowledge states, not automatically continuity errors.

The guide’s earlier wording “why he believed the death was deliberate” was ambiguous enough to suggest murder too early. It has been clarified to describe Monty’s account of a prepared ritual death, rather than assigning him later knowledge. Chapter 2 still needs to make the concealment/reporting motive legible.
