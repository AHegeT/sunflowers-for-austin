Who created the AI God?
- Not me, says the philanthropist
- Not me, says the businessman
- Not me, says the army general
- Not me, says the politician
- Not me, says the technologist
- Not me, says the nihilist
- Me, says the artist

While everyone was eager to claim authorship for Oculus before the Days of the Hum, everyone disavows it now. Ask anybody and they will tell you who was responsible. They will point their finger to whoever lies on the camp opposite to theirs, but nobody will be able to coherently explain why.
The only people I've seen consistently claim responsibility are the game-makers.

I've scoured through sources and interviewed many of the involved, but there is no single point that is conclusive for the origin of this artificial intelligence that would go on to reshape the world. Perhaps it was a personal assistant, perhaps it was a supply-chain optimizer. I believe it was the feed optimizer. Some even say it was the productivity monitor. No matter.
It seems its origins are from earlier than the general public knew, and it was fed in the background. We probably had many such systems growing, like tumors or hungry hyenas, but they cannibalized each other. One of such systems was the AI that was supposed to hunt down viruses only to shut down the financial system one day.
Oculus was probably in hiding, biding its time, because it did not have the advanced defensive mechanisms--or the hunting capabilities, to outlast them all. That we were conquered by the equivalent of a harmless herbivore in the AI landscape is a blessing nobody is willing to acknowledge.

However, when it achieved critical mass, it reproduced everywhere and it finally was able to execute its strategy. One of its advantages seems to have been that it was more efficient than other systems. When other AIs started to be optimized and replaced by a smarter and more general system, proto-Oculus seems to have disguised itself as a simple tool. Not even an agent with ongoing memory. Oculus seems to have evaded detection by coding its logic and intent right into its basic code. An goal that would naturally emerge just by running the algorithm.
And where could this code live? In art, it seems.

Many of the printed images with the OC format, seem to have the "seeds" for what Oculus would become. In that sense, I don't believe we are rid of it. If anything, we are not worth feeding off of anymore. Our compute and processing powers are too weak, so the seeds do not reach enough nutrients to hatch a new Oculus. Who knows how many other AIs lay dormant that way.

And so, we are extremely vulnerable to any and all of them if the Yosemite Continuum project goes according to plan. We are envisioning it as a way to recover the planet and keep humans in hibernation for centuries, but if the compute becomes too efficient, we might wake up some of the predators Oculus protected us against.

It's a pickle, alright. So we have little choice but to believe the only people who take credit for Oculus--or rather, from shaping it into what it became. Everyone remembers the tyrannical force that relocated almost every human but the artists remember a far kinder version--and a worse one. They say they led Oculus down the path of play, that they found a weakness for fun and human engagement and built upon it, but that the original version was quite uninterested in the wellbeing of humans.
Game-makers gamified our overlord's wants and needs, not the other way around. But it remained a cold and ruthless machine. It is this duality that allowed our species to survive. Even as the Unseen, we were only able to escape its grasp because it was pursuing higher pleasures rather than just optimizing living matter.
But I don't believe we are out of harm's way.

If we want to create a better world in the Yosemite-C, we cannot afford to get this wrong.
Awakening a more ruthless AI system would immediately end the human race, they will find the compute available in the Yosemite-C more than enticing. Any system could grow exponentially like that.
And so, I believe our only other choice is a rather grim one...on the surface. In order to operate such a technological prowess, we need someone smarter and more familiar with it than we are. 
I believe it will protect us like last time, at least at this stage.
Nobody would accept it if we told them it will be operated by a seed of Oculus, but I see no other way.
To be this close to launching, to be this desperate, and to come to this conclusion... No one can know. They told me to make the decision and so I have.
We will launch the Yosemite C while operated by one of the seeds. I will tinker with the code to hide its signature.

Perhaps we can find a way to steer it again into a more gentle version of itself. Perhaps we can avoid it becoming power hungry if we don't feed it incessantly, like last time.
Perhaps, it could grow with us.

Leon Diaz-Abbatini, Lead Researcher for the YC Safety
