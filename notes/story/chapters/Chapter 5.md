She felt a tornado all around her. The more she tried to focus the worse it became. She needed to ground herself, she knew that, but...there was something pulling at her. She could feel it.
She felt a tug on her hand and remembered to open up her eyes.
The little creature was looking at her with its big, round eyes. Like it was begging for something.
N-Oh, I'm sorry, little guy? Did I forget your meal time again? I'll get right on it...

Natalia had been having a hard time trying to focus on anything that wasn't that mental image of Leon during the funeral. She recognized that man, she knew that man. But how?
Her 10 years inside the Yosemite-C were lost to her, as they were to everyone else who had uploaded their consciousness. She knew through the various studies she read that there was nothing to rediscover, trying to recall those memories was a sure-fire way to trigger a TDS episode. The temporal lobe would flash like a lightning storm trying to find any leads, and then go dark.
But the feeling...there was something there. A faint trace that connected her to her old mentor and the meaning he held. "Or perhaps I'm nearing psychosis".
Codi tugged at her arm once more, who knows how much time had passed.

His care was simple enough: He was able to eat solids now and not just bathe in the soup of nutrients. He would need some time in the sun and some play. It was easy, but she often needed reminders from Paloma or now, fortunately, from the compakin himself. She had mixed feelings about it being bonded to her. For starters, she couldn't decide if she saw it as an "it" or a "him". She felt uncomfortable with how closely it walked the line between a newborn puppy and a human baby. She knew she didn't want kids and all the pets she'd had belonged to someone else in reality. She was always the aunt, the friend, the woman at the dog park. But this Codi felt hers and hers alone. Did Leon leave it for her? Did he know she'd find it if he tasked her with taking over the class?
She felt the world around her start to dissolve and the wind rising once again.
P-You better not be tripping again! If Codi hasn't had dinner yet, you're in for a scolding!

Natalia immediately opened her eyes and rushed to give Codie the pack of cookies, while her mind was still half-gone.

P-I knew it. I thought "no, she's a grown woman, of course she doesn't need the literal child to come remind her". But what is the literal child doing again?
N-I'm sorry...
P-Don't apologize to me, apologize to the baby.

Paloma held up Codi and pushed his face against Natalia's.
P-Isn't he literally perfect? Who's a perfect synthetic creature, huh??
N-He's...yeah, something.
P-A compakin, clearly.
N-I'm sorry, I'm just trying to make sense of Leon's death. If he didn't do it, then...
P-Yeah, he was killed. Obviously.
N-It could also have been of natural causes.
P-Right, he ingested the mushrooms that grew inside his body and eventually covered him head to toe. So that's why you shouldn't eat moldy bread. Silly him.
N-Okay, it's very unlikely he died of natural causes and, despite the many depressive notes around his lab, he didn't commit suicide. Do I have anything to back this up aside from...a feeling?
P-The best detectives already trust their gut! And mine is telling me that a killer always comes back to the crime scene!
N-If that's the case, we're out of luck. Who knows how many people were here during the funeral.
P-I know! Between 7 and 20. I put up a motion sensor and it roughly counts to that. Unless some people left and came back...it could also count dogs. Do you think there were any dogs in here...?
N-I don't. I also don't think that narrows it down. Say it was one of the Unseen or the guests. Couldn't they just send a handler to take care of it for them?
P-Nah, this was personal. And against my favorite or second favorite teacher nonetheless! Once I find the culprit, I'm going to punch him SO hard...Perhaps I already did.
N-Arcturus does seem to have a motive and he is very cutthroat. But would he go as far as to kill a person or have them killed?
P-Tich, you cannot use words like cuttrhoat to describe innocent people. Besides, that guy makes all my wad alerts go off.
N-Your what?
P-Wad.
N-Huh?
P-Ugh.

Paloma turns on her haptic glove to try to explain the concept better. It did a better job explaining it to Natalia, but she turned off her t-box just in case.
P-A cab.
N-Oh!
P-Like a cabron but not even deserving of that title? Like a sorry excuse for a cabron?
N-I see, the t-box was translating it as "wad". I guess a fuckwad.
P-You hadn't had to turn off your t-box in a while. Your Spanish is getting better. 
N-My t-box is.
P- Well, I'm glad you're learning and letting the neural networks do their job. They need a strong incentive to realign their weights.
N-Clear also does it.

Paloma and Natalia kept at it for a little longer, while Codie looked at them.
